# Hyperfine structure of the 4f²6s ²P J = 1/2 level (IDEN2 row 1089)

Status, 2026-09-17: **marked for analysis, nothing changed.** The level is
fixed in IDEN2 at 50869.296 cm⁻¹, which the evidence below says is its
**lower** hyperfine component, and the lines assigned to it are assigned
there. No measured wavenumber and no level position has been edited for this
note, and nothing has been run. This file records the evidence and the two
things that are not yet settled.

This is the companion of `4f26s_4P_0.5_hfs.md`, which does the same for row
1093. The two levels are the same pair of states seen from the two sides:
1093 is 84 % 4f²(³P₀)6s (0,1/2) with 14 % 4f²(³P₁)6s (1,1/2), and 1089 is
85 % (1,1/2) with 14 % (0,1/2).

## Terms

Everything used here, in full; nothing is assumed from the 1093 file.

* **Level 1089.** The row of `IDEN2/enlev.dat` labelled `f26s _3P2P`, J = 1/2,
  calculated at 50941.3 cm⁻¹ and adopted at 50869.296 cm⁻¹. Its pipeline
  identifier is `059003.000190` (`IDEN2/IDEN_level_ids.txt`). Unlike 1093 it
  is a level of long standing: it is already in the level list, in LOPT, and
  in the classification.
* **Composition** (`levels_pub.xlsx`): 85 % 4f²(³P₁)6s (1,1/2) and
  14 % 4f²(³P₀)6s (0,1/2). The bracket names the state of the 4f² core and
  then the pair (core J, 6s j) that is coupled to the level's J.
* **Hyperfine structure (hfs).** The ¹⁴¹Pr nucleus, the only stable one, has
  spin I = 5/2. A level of electronic angular momentum J couples with it to a
  total F running from |J − I| to J + I. For J = 1/2 there are exactly two
  sublevels, F = 2 and F = 3. Every "level" in the line list is really this
  pair, and a transition is really a pair or a group of lines.
* **A, the magnetic-dipole hyperfine constant**, in cm⁻¹. The sublevel
  energies are `E(F) = E_c + (A/2)·[F(F+1) − I(I+1) − J(J+1)]`. For J = 1/2
  that is **E(F=3) = E_c + 1.25 A** and **E(F=2) = E_c − 1.75 A**. So the
  splitting E(F=3) − E(F=2) is **3A**, and F = 3 lies above F = 2 only when
  A is positive. A negative A puts F = 3 below.
* **Centre of gravity (c.g.), E_c.** The average of the sublevels weighted by
  their degeneracies 2F + 1, here (7·E(F=3) + 5·E(F=2)) / 12. This is the
  number a published level list should carry, and it is what an unresolved
  line measures (see the next item).
* **7 : 5.** When one partner of a transition has J = 1/2 and the other
  partner's own hfs is not resolved, the two components have intensities in
  the ratio of the J = 1/2 level's degeneracies, F = 3 : F = 2 = 7 : 5. A
  useful consequence: the intensity-weighted mean position of the two
  components is exactly the position computed from the two centres of
  gravity. An unresolved blend therefore measures the c.g., not either
  component.
* **Character.** The one- or two-letter code in `IDEN2/dlv.dat` describing the
  recorded feature: `w` wide, `d` diffuse, `c` complex, `*r` a line already
  used in the fit (the asterisk), `r` its own tag. `w`, `d` and `c` are the
  observer's note that the feature is broader than the instrument, which is
  what an unresolved hfs group looks like.
* **Ritz position.** The wavenumber a transition should have, computed from
  the two adopted level energies. In `IDEN2/trans.dat` it is the fourth
  number of a `+` row; the recorded wavenumber, when one is marked, is the
  sixth, and the seventh is observed minus Ritz.
* **Adopted component.** Because a level list holds one energy per level, a
  level with resolved hfs has to be entered at one of its sublevels. 1089 is
  entered at the lower of its two, and every Ritz position in this file is
  computed from that.

## 1. What is seen

**One resolved pair.** Row 960 (`f26p ~3P4D`, J = 3/2, 80163.983) is the only
upper level whose line to 1089 is split into two recorded features:

| σ (cm⁻¹) | I | unc | character | σ − Ritz | dlv row |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 29294.708 | 11911 | 0.100 | `*r` | +0.021 | 5061 |
| 29294.209 | 10206 | 0.026 | — | −0.478 | 5062 |

The Ritz position from the adopted 1089 is 29294.687. The upper of the two,
the one already assigned, is the adopted component; the second lies
**0.499 cm⁻¹ lower in wavenumber**, that is **0.499 cm⁻¹ higher in energy**,
because 1089 is the lower level of this transition and a higher lower-level
energy gives a smaller wavenumber.

**Four unresolved blends.** Four more upper levels have a recorded feature
near the predicted position that is *not* assigned to 1089 and carries a
width character:

| upper row | label | Ritz | σ observed | I | character | σ − Ritz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 939 | `f26p ~3P2P`, J = 3/2 | 33934.838 | 33934.582 | 5435 | `w` | −0.256 |
| 940 | `f26p ~3P2P`, J = 1/2 | 33722.745 | 33722.470 | 20521 | `w` | −0.275 |
| 964 | `f26p ~3P2S`, J = 1/2 | 28509.103 | 28508.822 | 15897 | `d` | −0.281 |
| 972 | `f26p ~1D2P`, J = 1/2 | 27443.569 | 27443.332 | 4544 | `d` | −0.237 |

All four sit between the two components — mean −0.262, spread ±0.019 — and all
four are recorded as wide or diffuse. That is what an unresolved pair
0.48 cm⁻¹ apart looks like on this plate, and it is why they were left
unassigned: a Ritz test against the adopted component alone rejects them.

**One transition downwards.** 1089 → 1164 (`4f3 ~2D2D2`, J = 3/2, 26921.474)
has Ritz 23947.822 and no recorded line there. There is one at 23948.271,
**+0.449** — for a transition in which 1089 is now the *upper* level, a higher
1089 sublevel gives a *larger* wavenumber, so this is again the second,
higher-energy component, and again at about +0.48.

**The nine lines already assigned** to 1089 lie at −0.188, −0.112, −0.068,
−0.053, −0.005, +0.021, +0.039, +0.091 and +0.121 from Ritz, four of them
tagged `*r` and two `c`. This scatter, ±0.1 on lines quoted to ±0.01, is
itself a symptom: some of these are partly blended pairs pulled off the
component the level is entered at.

## 2. The splitting

Three independent readings of the same quantity:

* the resolved pair of 960: **0.499**;
* the shift of the 1164 line: **0.449**;
* the blends, which measure the c.g. and so give the splitting only together
  with the sign — see §3.

Taking the first two, the splitting is **0.47 ± 0.03 cm⁻¹**, so
**|A| = 0.157 ± 0.010 cm⁻¹**. The uncertainty is dominated by the difference
between the two, not by the line positions, which are good to 0.01–0.1.

## 3. The sign, and where the level really is

Two arguments, and they agree.

**Intensity.** F = 3 carries 7/12 of the strength and F = 2 carries 5/12, so
the stronger component is F = 3. In the 960 pair the adopted, lower-energy
component is the stronger: 11911 against 10206, a ratio of 1.17 where 7 : 5
predicts 1.40 and 5 : 7 predicts 0.71. The observed ratio is far from 0.71.
**The lower-energy component is F = 3**, which by the rule E(F=3) = E_c + 1.25A
means **A is negative**.

**The blend centroids, checked against row 964.** An unresolved pair sits at
the difference of the two centres of gravity (the 7 : 5 item in *Terms*). The
Ritz position, on the other hand, is the difference of the two adopted
positions. So for each blend

  σ_obs − Ritz = −(E_up,adopted − E_c,up) + (E_1089,adopted − E_c,1089).

Row 964 is the one upper level whose own offset is known: its hyperfine
constant was measured from the 1093 lines as A = +0.084 ± 0.009
(`4f26s_4P_0.5_hfs.md` §6) and it is entered in IDEN2 at its F = 3 sublevel,
so E_up,adopted − E_c,up = +1.25 × 0.084 = +0.105. With σ_obs − Ritz = −0.281
that gives

  E_1089,adopted − E_c,1089 = −0.176.

If the adopted component were F = 2 this would have to equal −1.75 A with
|A| = 0.157, that is −0.275 — too far out. If it is F = 3 it equals
+1.25 A = −0.196 for A = −0.157, which matches −0.176 within the
uncertainties of both A values. Again **the adopted component is F = 3 and A
is negative**. The same calculation run backwards on the other three blends
puts each of those upper levels 0.04–0.08 cm⁻¹ above its own c.g., small
positive offsets of exactly the size expected if they too are entered at an
F = 3 sublevel of a small positive A — which is what the 4f²6p levels have.

So, provisionally:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| adopted position, E(F = 3) | 50869.296 |
| A | **−0.157 ± 0.010** |
| E(F = 2) = E(F=3) − 3A | 50869.77 |
| centre of gravity E_c = E(F=3) − 1.25 A | **50869.49 ± 0.02** |

The c.g. is about **0.20 cm⁻¹ above** the position the level list carries.

## 4. Against the calculation

`A_hfs_levels.csv` gives `059003.000190` **A = −0.0787 ± 0.0022**, from
Reader & Sugar (1965). The sign agrees; the size is **half** the measured one,
a discrepancy of 35 σ on the quoted uncertainty. That uncertainty is not a
fair measure of the disagreement, for a reason particular to this level.

In a 4f²6s level the constant is a sum of a 4f part and a 6s part. For the
(1,1/2) component that dominates 1089 — core ³P₁ coupled with the 6s
electron's j = 1/2 to J = 1/2 — the 6s electron's projection factor is

  [J(J+1) + j(j+1) − J_c(J_c+1)] / [2J(J+1)] = (0.75 + 0.75 − 2)/1.5 = −1/3,

so the 6s contribution is −a_6s/3 = −0.213 cm⁻¹ with Reader & Sugar's
a_6s = 0.639. The 4f core contributes about +0.13 with the opposite sign, and
A is what is left of a cancellation between two terms each larger than the
result. A 10 % error in either term, or in the 14 % admixture of (0,1/2),
moves A by more than the whole difference between −0.079 and −0.157. **1089
is a sensitive test of the hyperfine parameters and of the composition, in a
way 1093 is not** — for 1093 the (0,1/2) part has a ³P₀ core with no magnetic
moment at all, the whole constant is 0.84 × a_6s, and calculation (+0.500) and
measurement (+0.505) agree to 1 %.

The pair is also worth treating together: 1089 and 1093 exchange the same two
basis states, so their two constants are not independent, and a two-level
model has to reproduce both. Measured: A(1093) = +0.505, A(1089) = −0.157.

## 5. Open points

1. **The 960 pair may not be clean.** Row 960 has J = 3/2, and
   `A_hfs_levels.csv` gives it A = +0.0395 ± 0.0168. For J = 3/2 the four
   sublevels F = 1…4 span 9A ≈ 0.36 cm⁻¹ — not negligible against the
   0.48 being measured. Each of the two features is then itself a group, and
   the 0.499 spacing and the 1.17 intensity ratio are both affected. The
   conclusion of §3 does not rest on the ratio being 1.40, only on its being
   well above 1, which a symmetric smearing does not change; but the 0.499
   should not be quoted as a precise splitting.
2. **The 1164 line is not understood.** The recorded feature at 23948.271 has
   intensity 124.4, and there is a second at 23947.123, 1.148 cm⁻¹ lower, with
   intensity 124.3 — an equal pair, and a third at 23946.642 with 1114.
   Nothing is recorded at the Ritz position 23947.822. If 23948.271 is 1089's
   F = 2 component then it is the *weaker* of the two (5/12), and the stronger
   F = 3 line is missing, which cannot be right on its own. 1164 is a 4f³
   level with J = 3/2 and has no entry in `A_hfs_levels.csv`; a hyperfine
   constant of its own of a few hundredths would rearrange this whole group.
   Until that is worked out, the 1164 line is evidence for the size of the
   splitting only, not for the sign.
3. **Nothing has been decided about moving the level.** Correcting 1089 from
   E(F = 3) to the c.g. would move it +0.20 cm⁻¹, and it is a level with nine
   lines already in the fit. The same question is open for 1093 and will be
   settled for both at once.

## 6. What would settle it

* A second resolved pair on a transition whose *upper* level has J = 1/2 and a
  small A — rows 940, 964 and 972 all qualify. On the present plate they are
  blends, so this means either a better spectrum or a deconvolution of the
  recorded profile.
* A_hfs for row 1164, which would let the 23948.271/23947.123 pair be read.
* The two-level fit of §4 across 1089 and 1093 together, which would say
  whether −0.157 is consistent with any reasonable a_4f, a_6s and mixing
  angle, or whether the composition is wrong.
